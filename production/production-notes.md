# AI Town Hall — methods and production notes

One assessment of the seven original replies supplied by Chris Golden, in screenshot order. There are no additional candidate questions or review rounds. The video covers all six saved reviews of each reply: 42 assessments in total.

## Inputs and scoring

All reviewers were instructed to use the original resident question, the same seven screenshots, rubric v2 and the same dated context packet. Candidate names were visible. Sol 6.1 and Astra were explicitly dispatched in fresh sessions; the user supplied Claude, Grok and Gemini outputs. Other reviewers’ scores were excluded from the review instructions. This is a common-input procedure, not candidate-name blinding or proof of statistically independent judgments.

Each criterion uses integer levels 0–4. Weighted points equal level ÷ 4 × criterion weight. The six weights are 20, 20, 15, 15, 20 and 10. Scores assess the supplied answer’s coverage, reasoning and demonstrated preparation; they do not establish overall candidate competence, require enthusiasm for AI, reward prose polish, or require agreement that job losses are inevitable.

All 42 totals and 252 weighted entries were checked against the source data. Grok’s supplied corrections raise Shanna Maltby to 91.25 and Jason Brass to 77.5. Grok chose level 4 for criterion 2 in each case; no automatic rounding was applied. All 252 levels now comply with the integer rubric. Other weighted points and narratives are unchanged. The original review and both supplied corrections are preserved. No panel mean is shown. Score ranges describe disagreement, not confidence intervals.

## What is performed

All robots are original code-drawn characters. Kokoro stock voices perform saved reviewer summaries, host presentation, closing synthesis and AI-written banter. Codex wrote the script and synthesized the closing advice from the saved reviews. Robot dialogue is not an official model voice or a live multi-model conversation. Every banter scene carries the visible label “AI-WRITTEN SCRIPTED BANTER · NOT A LIVE PANEL EXCHANGE” and credits Codex. The 20 banter lines were generated by the coordinating AI for this presentation and do not change any grade.

The introduction identifies all seven respondents and six reviewers, explains the shared-input method, displays all six weights and distinguishes scoring from candidate suitability. The finished presentation uses the final checked scores without discussing resolved scoring-format issues. The all-scores comparison table preserves screenshot order.

The expanded ending compares the four Ward 1 replies (two seats), the two Regional Councillor Wards 1 and 2 replies (one seat), and the supplied mayoral reply. We have not established a reason for nonresponse or audited the complete Facebook thread. Jason Brass is the only mayoral respondent in this exercise; the default participation result is not evidence of superiority over unseen answers or an election prediction.

The shared future gap, planning scenarios and seven candidate-specific closing recommendations are an AI-written synthesis by Codex of the original replies and saved reviews. They are not fresh independent reviews by the six panelists, model-endorsed consensus statements or additional candidate questions. Each recommendation’s six saved gap fields and editorial basis are recorded in ai-town-hall-closing-summary.json. Named robot voices perform these lines under the label “CLOSING ADVICE · AI-WRITTEN SYNTHESIS OF SAVED REVIEWS.”

The central synthesis is a timed, funded transition plan linking changes in work, household/business pressure, public-service demand and public finances before savings or new opportunities arrive. The three scenarios illustrate preparation for gradual adoption, rapid disruption and disappointing savings; they are recommendations for planning, not forecasts. Several replies already acknowledge disruption and uncertainty. A missing detail in a short reply does not establish absence of knowledge or a full policy platform.

## Images, sound and accessibility

Portraits were obtained from five candidate campaign websites, the Chamber candidate page for Mike Albano, and a supplied election-announcement portrait of Kevin Carruthers published by HaltonHillsToday. The full image is contained within each frame without retouching or invented expressions. Source links, retrieval dates and hashes are recorded in candidate-photo-sources.json and candidate-photo-sources.md. These image sources were researched for presentation only, not supplied as new grading evidence.

The video uses original synthesized musical cues and quiet score-reveal tones, without third-party music. Robot bodies float, eyes blink and mouth waveforms follow the actual scene audio. Scores fade in after the start of each assessment.

The delivery is one complete MP4 with English subtitles and fourteen chapters embedded. The production SRT and chapter guide remain as supporting records, not separate required viewing. Phrase timings are estimated within each scene from its actual audio duration; they are not forced word alignment. Candidate chapters, an all-scores chapter, three race comparisons, the shared future gap and closing advice are indexed in the MP4.
