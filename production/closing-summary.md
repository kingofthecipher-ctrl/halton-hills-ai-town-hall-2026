## What is missing most about the future?

The biggest shared future gap is the transition itself. Several replies acknowledge jobs, risk and uncertainty, so this is not a claim that they ignored disruption. But much of the response stays at the level of useful tools, efficiency, skills or historical reassurance. What is mostly missing is a timed plan connecting changing work, household pressure, service demand and public finances, including the period before promised savings or new opportunities arrive.

The advice is to prepare for more than one pace of change. For gradual adoption, measure real gains and build skills. For rapid disruption, track local pressure and coordinate a response with the governments responsible. If promised savings disappoint, protect priority services and use a funded fallback. These are planning scenarios, not forecasts or claims that job losses are inevitable.

This is editorial synthesis of the saved reviews and original replies, not a fresh output endorsed by all six models. A short reply cannot establish a candidate’s full knowledge or platform.

## Biggest advice for each respondent

### Shanna Maltby

For Shanna, the main opportunity is to make the process executable: name the first pilot, its owner, cost and deadline. Add a service-funding fallback if savings are small.

Basis: Pilot measurement and safeguards are already present; operational and fiscal detail would strengthen them.

### Kevin Carruthers

For Kevin, turn the workforce warning into local action. Pair it with a feasible Town initiative, a funding response and an account of relevant personal experience. Concern about AI is not itself a scoring fault.

### Mike Albano

For Mike, fill the gap between adopting useful tools and reaching the hoped-for new opportunities. Explain how services and affected people would be supported during that transition, with a concrete funding mechanism.

Basis: The repeated funding gap is not resolved by the historical analogy or a general efficiency condition.

### Jason Brass

For Jason, describe personal technology use and what it has taught you. Keep the practical trials, but specify safeguards and how services remain funded if the savings fall short.

Basis: Personal use is consistently identified as missing; safeguards and fallback finance recur in the saved reviews.

### Alex Hilson

For Alex, move from examples to measured outcomes: show costs, verified savings and oversight. Add a funding and workforce plan for change that arrives faster than the efficiency gains.

Basis: Reviews credit examples but distinguish claimed savings from evidence and flag transition funding and safeguards.

### Hillary Spurr

For Hillary, connect the people-first priorities to funded actions within the responsible government’s remit. Explain personal technology experience and how workers can adapt as tasks change, without assuming any occupation stays untouched.

Basis: Saved reviews identify personal use, implementation, funding and changes within valued occupations.

### Anthony Galati

For Anthony, cost a shorter-hours trial before relying on it. Demonstrate enough productivity to preserve pay and service coverage, define the Town’s role, and state the fallback if those gains do not appear.

Basis: Reviews repeatedly identify the assumed gains, financing, municipal role and service-coverage gaps.

## Practical close

Make readiness observable: action, owner, cost, deadline, measure, review and funded fallback. Treat fast disruption and disappointing savings as scenarios to prepare for, not inevitable outcomes.
