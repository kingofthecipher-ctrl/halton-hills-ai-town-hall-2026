## D. Major differences and limitations

**Major differences**
- **Method versus position.** Maltby and Brass give a testable method: pilots, measured results, named service areas and (in Maltby's case) safeguards. Albano, Spurr and Galati mostly state a stance. Carruthers states a concern, and Hilson gives examples.
- **Evidence of preparedness.** Hilson supplies the most specific awareness evidence (all unverified), while Galati's candid non-use is a different but real form of awareness.
- **The funding question.** Only Maltby links lost income to both taxpayer capacity and service demand while proposing local and cross-government steps. Brass also reasons about funding, but he omits worker transitions and the roles of other governments. Hilson, Albano and Spurr give little reasoning on funding. Galati and Carruthers reason about employer or household effects rather than public finances.
- **Analogies.** Albano, Hilson and Galati lean on history, which the shared context treats as supportive but not predictive.
- **Skepticism versus optimism.** Carruthers's cautious stance is not penalized for its position. His score reflects forecast certainty and thin mechanisms. Albano's optimism is scored on the same reasoning standard.

**Limitations**
- These are short social-media replies, and the rubric favors municipal-action content. Replies that frame the issue at a different level (Galati, Carruthers) score lower on criterion 4 by design.
- The assessment is not blinded, since names are visible, and I used no outside knowledge about any candidate.
