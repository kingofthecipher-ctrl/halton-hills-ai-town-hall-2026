# Rubric v2 and fairness instructions

Score each criterion at an INTEGER level from 0 to 4. Weighted points = level ÷ 4 × criterion weight. Use the same standard for all seven replies. Do not curve scores or force a ranking.

1. Answers the resident's questions — 20 points
   Does the reply directly address technological change and government adaptation, personal familiarity/use, and the jobs/public-funding question? Distinguish answering a question from merely mentioning its topic. Score coverage here; assess the quality of the reasoning in the relevant criteria below, without duplicating the same penalty.

2. Future reasoning and realism — 20 points
   Does the argument make sense for the coming four years and beyond? Is its technology/economic reasoning sound? Does it distinguish possibility from certainty, past analogy from future evidence, and different technologies where relevant? Does it recognize both plausible benefits and risks? Do not require accurate prediction of unknowable outcomes or mention of every technology.

3. Preparedness demonstrated by the reply — 15 points
   What evidence of relevant experience, learning, awareness of limits, use of expertise, or an ability to ask informed questions is actually supplied? Acknowledged non-use can still show honesty and awareness. Do not equate tool use, confidence, credentials, or enthusiasm with readiness. This score concerns demonstrated preparedness in the text, not the person's actual overall competence.

4. Practical local action and accountability — 15 points
   Is there a feasible municipal response, with useful priorities, steps, responsibilities, partnerships, evaluation, or timing? Can a resident see what would happen next and how success or failure would be assessed? Do not demand a complete costed platform from a short social-media reply; do distinguish an actionable direction from a slogan.

5. Jobs, public funding, and economic tradeoffs — 20 points
   Does the reply reason through possible changes in employment, household/business finances, public revenues, costs and service demand? Does it offer a plausible local approach or explain what needs other governments/employers? Are proposed savings, continued service delivery, and protections for workers justified rather than assumed? No particular economic policy is required for a high score.

6. Blind spots, safeguards, and affected people — 10 points
   Does the reply recognize relevant ways its own preferred approach could fail, who might be excluded or harmed, and proportionate safeguards? Depending on the proposal, this may include privacy, cybersecurity, errors, human oversight, accessibility, workforce transitions, vendor dependence, procurement costs, or a fallback if expected savings fail. Assess relevance, not how many checklist items are named. Identify your inferred omissions as omissions, not beliefs the candidate holds.

LEVEL ANCHORS

0 = not addressed or no usable support supplied.
1 = relevant acknowledgment with little reasoning or detail.
2 = meaningful partial answer with important gaps.
3 = clear, reasoned answer with useful specifics and manageable gaps.
4 = strong, coherent answer that substantively addresses the criterion and its relevant limits/tradeoffs.

FAIRNESS RULES

- Grade the response, not party, personality, prose polish, likability, or agreement with the resident or with you.
- As an AI reviewer, explicitly guard against favoring pro-AI positions or penalizing well-reasoned skepticism.
- Do not reward length, buzzwords, named tools, or optimism by themselves. A concise, well-reasoned reply can score highly.
- A reasoned challenge to the premise that job losses are inevitable can score highly. Do not require agreement with any preferred AI timeline, tax policy, or labour policy.
- Do not speculate about whether a reply was written using AI. Stylistic impressions are not evidence of authorship.
- Missing detail is not proof of ignorance, bad faith, or absence of a policy. Use “not demonstrated in this reply.” Do not invent positions or promises.
- Do not punish the same issue across several criteria without explaining its distinct effect. Keep criterion 1 about coverage.
- The candidates' names are visible in the screenshots, so this is not a blinded assessment. Do not claim otherwise. Use no outside knowledge about them.
- If a screenshot is materially cropped or unreadable, identify the missing text and withhold that response's total rather than guess or score the missing material as zero. If all visible response text is readable, proceed provisionally; you cannot establish that all thread follow-ups were supplied.
- Use confidence labels to reflect evidence limitations. These are qualitative labels, not probabilities. Do not invent or guess the exact model version running this chat; the human will record the model label from the app.
