## D. Major Differences and Limitations

**Differences:**

- **Coverage:** Only Maltby addressed all three parts of the resident's question substantively. Brass and Hilson covered two parts well. The other four left the public-funding question largely unanswered, which was the most common gap.
- **Approach to adaptation:** Replies ranged from structured pilot-and-measure processes (Maltby, Brass), to inventories of existing initiatives (Hilson), to general endorsements (Albano), to labour-focused positions (Spurr's essential workers, Galati's shorter week, Carruthers' concern about job losses).
- **Stance on AI:** Skepticism (Carruthers, partly Galati) wasn't penalized as a stance. Those replies lost points for missing local steps and funding reasoning, the same way optimistic replies (Albano, Hilson) did.
- **Use of history:** Albano, Hilson, and Galati drew on historical precedent. None examined how the current transition might differ from past ones.

**Limitations:**

- This assessment uses only the screenshots and supplied context, with no browsing. Many personal and experience claims remain unverified.
- Thread replies were not supplied (Spurr shows "View 1 reply"), so all totals are provisional.
- Mayoral and ward candidates are compared on a single rubric, even though their roles differ.
- Short social-media replies limit what can be demonstrated, so a low score means "not demonstrated in this reply," not an absence of competence or policy.
- Rubric levels involve judgment, and a different reviewer could reasonably differ by a level on several criteria. These scores are not objective truths.
- The assessment is not blinded, because the names were visible.
